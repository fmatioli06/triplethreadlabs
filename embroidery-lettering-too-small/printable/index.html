LETTERING SIZE CHECK

Two minutes before the real garment. Measure the smallest letter, compare it to the floor for its style, and let a test stitch of just the words settle it.
Below the floor, the letter is a blob. The fix is bigger or bolder, never a setting.

THE FLOOR, AS A STARTING POINT

  • Block lettering: trouble starts under about 5 mm tall (roughly 0.2 in) on most fabrics.
  • Script and serif: close to double that. Thin strokes and fine details need room.
  • Raised, textured, or structured fabric pushes the floor up. Pile pushes it most.
  • Starting points, not guarantees. The test stitch on the real fabric settles it.

BEFORE YOU RUN IT

  • Smallest letter measured at finished size, not zoomed in on screen.
  • The openings in a, e, and o are real openings, not slivers.
  • Letters have air between them, and stacked lines are not packed tight.
  • Test stitch of just the words, same fabric, same backing.
  • The sample reads at arm's length. If it fills in there, the file changes first.

IF IT FILLED IN: FOUR MOVES

  1. Go a little bigger. One bump in height widens every opening at once.
  2. Switch to a bolder, simpler style. Same words, sturdier letters.
  3. Add spacing. Letter spacing first, line spacing on stacked lines.
  4. Drop the smallest line. The tagline is the usual volunteer.

FONT CHOICE AT SMALL SIZES

  • Block is the small-size default: even strokes, open openings, nothing fine to lose.
  • Serif needs a size where its thin strokes and little feet can exist.
  • Script needs the most room of all. Save it for big placements.
  • Narrow and decorative fonts squeeze the openings. Judge them at finished size.
  • Borderline job? Ask for the lettering digitized for a lighter weight thread, then re-test.
Measure it. Test it. Read it at arm's length.
