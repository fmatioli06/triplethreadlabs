Why Is My Bobbin Thread Showing on Top of My Embroidery?

The short answer

Bobbin thread showing on top means the top thread is winning the pull. The stitch should lock inside the fabric, and instead the bobbin thread is being pulled up where you can see it. That can be tension, but it is often lint in the bobbin case, bobbin thread that missed its spring, a snag in the top path, or one dense section pulling harder than the rest. Check the bobbin side first, rethread the top, test on scrap, and move tension last, in small steps.

You lift the hoop and there it is: white flecks or a pale line through color that should be solid. In the groups the replies split right away, half say loosen the top tension, half say tighten the bobbin. In my shop I do neither first. Where it shows usually tells you more than any dial will.

The short answer

Work this order. 1. The bobbin side: clean, reseat, and make sure the bobbin thread is routed the way your manual shows. 2. The top path: rethread in full and clear any snag. 3. Needle and thread: fresh needle, and a hard look at anything you just changed. 4. The design: check whether the showing lives in one dense section or one color. 5. Tension: only after the first four, on a test stitch, in small steps.

The small win: before you change any setting, stitch one satin column on scrap, flip it, and look at the back. That tells you whether the pull is off everywhere or only in one place, and that decides the fix.

Read where it shows before you touch a dial

Bobbin showing is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Three patterns cover most group questions, and each points somewhere different.

It shows everywhere, in every color. That points at the shared parts of the stitch: the bobbin side, the top path as a whole, or the overall pull balance. Start with Checks 1 and 2.

It shows in one color or one section only. That points away from a general setting. A dial does not know which color is stitching. Suspect that spool, that needle and path, or that section of the design, especially if those sections use different thread or stitch types.

It shows on satin but not on fills. Satin pulls hard in a narrow band, so it shows a small imbalance first. The problem may still be in the fills, just hidden. That is why the test below uses satin.

The pattern to remember: everywhere means shared setup. One color or one section means that spool, that path, or that part of the design. Neither pattern starts with a dial.

The test stitch that tells the truth

Stitch a short satin column on scrap in the same fabric family as the real job, then flip it over and read the back.

The picture you want: bobbin thread down the middle of the back, top thread wrapping evenly around both sides. If bobbin is crowding the front, the top side is pulling it up. If the back shows almost no bobbin and the front looks loose, the pull is off the other way.

Keep that scrap. Test every fix the same way, one change at a time, so you can see which change moved the picture.

Check 1: The bobbin side first

Start underneath. A bobbin that offers no resistance will get pulled up no matter what the top side does.

Take the bobbin case out the way your manual shows and clean it. Lint caught where the bobbin thread passes can hold it off its proper path or let it slip loose. Brush the area out, check for scraps under the plate, and look at the case for nicks while it is in your hand.

Then route the bobbin thread again, slowly. On most machines it has to pass under a small tension spring or through a guide point in the case. Miss that step and the bobbin feeds with almost no hold, so the top thread pulls it straight up. It is an easy miss after a quick bobbin change, and it looks exactly like a tension problem.

Check the bobbin itself too: seated fully, turning the direction your manual shows, and wound evenly, not soft and spongy. A badly wound bobbin feeds unevenly, which can show as flecks that come and go inside one design.

Check 2: The top thread path

Now rethread the top in full, from the spool. Walk every guide in order the way your manual draws it. On my BAi Mirror the habit is the same as it was on my single needle machines: one missed guide changes how hard that needle pulls.

While you rethread, look for snags. Thread caught on the spool edge, pulling off unevenly, or wrapped where it should not be can make the top thread arrive in jerks. A jerky pull wins in bursts, and bobbin thread shows in bursts with it.

If the showing is in one color only, this check is where it usually lives. Swap that spool, or move that color to a different needle path on a multi-needle, and test again. If the showing follows the spool, you have your answer.

Check 3: Needle and thread

Fit a fresh needle. A dull, bent, or rough eyed needle drags on the thread, and drag on the top side changes the pull. A suspect needle makes every other check harder to read, so do not leave one in while you test.

Then think about what changed. A lot of group questions start the same way: it was fine, then I changed something, then the bobbin started showing. A new needle, a different thread, a fresh bobbin, a switch back from sewing. Whatever changed last is a suspect. Swap that one part back and run the satin test again.

Check 4: The design and the fabric

If the first three checks are clean, look at what you are asking the stitch to do.

Dense sections pull harder. A tight satin column or packed lettering will pull bobbin thread up sooner than a loose fill on the same settings. If the showing lives only in the densest part of one design, and a different design stitches clean on the same scrap, the file is part of the story. Same problem on a known good file points at your side, problem only on one file points at the file.

Fabric plays a part too. A thin or stretchy fabric gives the stitch less to lock inside, so the lock can ride up more easily. The useful move is the test: same design on scrap in the same fabric family, read the back. If it only shows on the thin garment, you have learned something a dial could not tell you.

If it is the file, send whoever digitized it a note naming the exact section where the bobbin shows and what you already cleaned and tested. That gets a better revision than a general complaint.

Check 5: Tension, last and in small steps

Only if the showing is everywhere after the first four checks do you look at tension. By now you have ruled out the causes it usually gets blamed for.

Note your starting setting so you can go back to it. Then make one small step, run the satin test, and read the back. One step, one test. Big turns teach you nothing, and they are how a small pull problem becomes loose stitching on the front too.

Honest limit: no dial numbers or bobbin case turns here, because those differ by machine. Your manual is the source for how yours adjusts. If a machine has stitched badly ever since a service, with tension already turned both ways, work this order once and, if the test still shows bobbin everywhere, call a technician.

The two minute version

  1. Read the pattern. Everywhere, one color only, or satin only. Each points somewhere different.
  2. Run a satin test on scrap and flip it. Bobbin thread should sit down the middle of the back.
  3. Clean and reroute the bobbin side. Lint out, thread routed per your manual, bobbin seated and evenly wound.
  4. Rethread the top in full. Clear snags, and swap the spool if only one color shows.
  5. Fresh needle, and undo the last change if the problem started right after one.
  6. Tension last. Note the start, one small step, one test, repeat only if needed.

Straight answers

Is bobbin thread showing on top always a tension problem?
No. Tension is one cause, but it is rarely the first one to check. Lint in the bobbin case, bobbin thread that missed its tension spring, a snag in the top thread path, or a dense section of the design can all pull bobbin thread up. Work the bobbin side and the top path first, test on scrap, and only move tension in small steps if the showing is still there.
Why is bobbin thread showing in only one color or one section?
That points away from a general setting. If one color shows and the rest stitch clean, suspect that spool, that path, or that section. Swap the spool or rethread that path, and check whether that section is dense satin or small lettering, which pulls harder than a loose fill.
Why does bobbin thread show on satin columns but not on fills?
A satin column is a tight back and forth pull in a narrow band, so a small pull imbalance shows there first. A fill spreads the same pull over a wider area and can hide it. Read a satin test column on scrap when you test a fix, because a fill can look fine while the problem is still there.
I turned the tension and it got worse. What should I do?
Go back to the setting you started from if you noted it, then stop turning. Clean the bobbin area, make sure the bobbin thread is routed the way your manual shows, rethread the top in full, and run a fresh satin test on scrap. If clean, correctly routed, and correctly threaded still shows bobbin everywhere, that is when a small tension step, one at a time with a test after each, is worth trying.
