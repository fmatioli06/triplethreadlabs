THREAD BREAK CHECK ORDER

The break is a symptom, not the problem. Work the order top to bottom, one change and one test at a time, and stop the moment the run goes clean. Tension is last on purpose.
Path first. Needle second. Thread third. Design fourth. Tension last.

READ THE BREAK FIRST

  • Frayed, fuzzy end: friction wore it down. Needle eye, a burr, a rough guide.
  • Clean snap: a sudden pull, or a weak spot that gave way at once.
  • Same spot, every run: that spot is the problem. Think file.
  • Breaks that wander: the setup. Path, needle, or spool.

CHECK 1: THE THREAD PATH

  • Rethread the whole path from the spool. Never pull a knot through the guides.
  • Thread with the tension discs open, per your manual, so the thread seats.
  • Every guide in the path. Missed the take-up lever? It breaks or loops at once.
  • Feel for burrs and rough spots. Check the spool edge and the spool notch.
  • Clear lint and adhesive build-up around the guides and needle plate.

CHECK 2: THE NEEDLE

  • Change it. A fresh needle costs cents and fixes an embarrassing share of breaks.
  • Hit a hoop, a pin, or a thick seam? That needle is done, even if it looks straight.
  • Right type for the fabric: ballpoint for knits, sharp for wovens.
  • Seated all the way up, turned the way your manual shows.
  • Heavy thread sawing at the eye? Bigger eye, not more tension.

CHECK 3: THE THREAD ITSELF

  • Swap in a spool you trust and run the same design.
  • Breaks stop: the old spool was the problem. Breaks stay: keep going down the order.
  • Old cones dry out and weaken. Cheap thread is thin in spots, and the thin spot snaps.
  • Damaged cone or spool edge catches on every turn.
  • Metallic and specialty threads are fussier: fresh needle, clean path, slower run.

CHECK 4: THE DESIGN

  • Same spot every time, with a clean path, fresh needle, and trusted spool: it is the file.
  • Stitches packed too tight or too short in one area: the needle hammers the same holes.
  • Slow down over the spot. Slowing fixes it means the area runs at the edge.
  • Shrunk a design without re-digitizing? Same stitches, less space, more breaks.
  • Send the digitizer the exact spot and what it does there. Ask them to open up that area.

CHECK 5: TENSION, LAST

  • Only when checks 1 to 4 are clean. Tension rarely goes wrong by itself.
  • Test on scrap: stitch a satin column and read the back.
  • The picture you want: bobbin thread down the middle, top thread wrapping evenly.
  • Bobbin thread showing on top means the top is pulling too hard.
  • Small steps, test after each, stop when it reads right. Know your normal setting so you can come back to it.
Same spot, blame the spot. Wandering breaks, blame the setup. One change, one test.
