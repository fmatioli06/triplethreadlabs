CAP RUN CHECKLIST

One sacrificial cap before the order. Hold the panel firm, keep the design low and on the seam, and read the test cap by where it failed before any customer hat goes on the frame.
A cap front is a curve, not a flat panel. Hold it, place it, and test it like one.

BEFORE YOU MOUNT

  • Design fits the panel: starting point around two inches tall or less, sitting low just above the brim seam. The test cap settles the number.
  • Center of the front panel marked. The design answers to the center seam, not the brim.
  • Backing cut to cover the whole design area with margin, and placed so it cannot creep mid-run.
  • Structured job, structured test cap. Unstructured job, unstructured test cap. Same style, same everything.
  • Fresh needle in. Cap breaks cluster on tired needles and seam crossings.

MOUNT AND HOLD

  • Panel smoothed onto the form evenly, no slack pooled at one side. Bunched left now means drifted left later.
  • Snug, not stretched. Pulled out of shape mounting, it relaxes mid-run and takes the design with it.
  • Whole cap secured: sweatband, strap, or clips set, not just the front held.
  • Design area inside the frame window with room to spare. Finger across the panel: uniformly firm, no soft spots.
  • Judge it mounted, at eye level, the way it will be worn. Centered on the seam, sitting low.

RUN THE TEST CAP

  • Full setup, nothing different from the order: same backing, same placement marks, same design.
  • Watch and listen at the center seam crossing. That is where the needle works hardest.
  • Note exactly where anything fails before you touch a setting. Location first, changes second.
  • One change, one retest. Three changes at once and the clean test teaches you nothing.

READ THE TEST

  • Puckers crowding the top corners: the design is climbing the curve, or the panel crept. Lower it, shrink it slightly, or fix the hold.
  • Breaks or bends at the center: the seam stack deflecting the needle. A seam problem, not a tension problem.
  • Design straight but shifted or tilted: the cap moved in the frame. The hold failed quietly.
  • Wobble in the middle letters only: the design shifted climbing over the seam. A seam and file conversation.
  • Clean, centered, flat: setup proven. Mount every customer cap exactly the way you mounted the test.
Cheap cap first, customer caps second. One change, one test.
