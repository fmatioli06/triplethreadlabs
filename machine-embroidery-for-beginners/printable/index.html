FIRST STITCH-OUT CHECKLIST

New machine, first design. Short supply list, careful hoop, slow run, then read the back before you judge the front.
Hooping and stabilizer first. Settings last. One change, one test.

THE SHORT LIST

  • The machine and the hoop that came in the box.
  • One pack of medium cutaway stabilizer.
  • Embroidery thread, and a plain cotton blank you do not care about: tea towel, tote, quilting cotton.
  • Small scissors or snips for thread tails.
  • Can wait: thread libraries, specialty hoops, sprays, software.

BEFORE YOU PRESS START

  • Bobbin seated the way your manual shows, turning the right direction.
  • Thread the top in full, every guide in order. One missed guide is the top cause of first-run tangles.
  • Pick a small design built into the machine. No tiny lettering yet.
  • Slow the machine down before the run, not after a problem.

HOOPING

  • Stabilizer under the fabric, both hooped together.
  • Snug and flat, like a drum head. Never stretched tight.
  • No stabilizer means puckering, however thick the fabric feels.

RUNNING

  • Stay for the first minute: thread feeding smooth, no loops on top, no odd sounds, fabric staying flat.
  • Looks wrong in minute one: stop and look. Hoping is how tangles become bird nests.
  • Judge the piece out of the hoop, laid flat, not while it is still hooped.

READ THE BACK FIRST

  • Healthy: bobbin thread sits down the middle of the satin edges on the back.
  • Tangled wad underneath: threading. Rethread in full before anything else.
  • Fabric puckered or wavy: hooping or stabilizer. Snugger hoop, heavier stabilizer.
  • White thread on top: bobbin seated wrong or a snag in the top path. Not a tension dial, yet.

THE FIRST WEEK RULE

  • Same small design, same fabric, several runs.
  • Change exactly one thing between runs: hoop, stabilizer, or speed.
  • Every run then teaches you what that one change does.
Most first stitch-outs that look broken are hooping or stabilizer. Check the hoop before any setting.
