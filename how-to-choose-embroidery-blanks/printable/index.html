BLANK TEST CHECKLIST

Run this on every new blank before it goes on a quote. Three reads, one test stitch, one wash. A blank is only chosen after it passes.
Read the fabric. Check the surface. Walk the construction. Then prove it on a spare.

READ THE FABRIC

  • Stretch: pull both ways. Gives and springs back means knit, whatever the tag says. Holds its shape means woven.
  • Body: enough weight to carry stitches, or thin and drapey enough to show every pull?
  • Weave: tight and even, or loose and slubby? Fine detail needs the tight floor.

CHECK THE SURFACE

  • Smooth and flat: the easy case. What you hoop is what you get.
  • Pile (fleece, towels): stitches will sink. Plan the topping setup before you commit.
  • Slippery or coated: expect creep in the hoop. Test the placement, not just the design.
  • Deep dye: dark blanks can bleed onto light thread in the wash. The wash test decides.

WALK THE CONSTRUCTION

  • Seams in or near the design area?
  • Pockets or a placket fixing your placement?
  • Zippers, snaps, or buttons inside the hoop field?
  • Hems, cuffs, or sleeves too narrow to hoop flat?
  • A lining that can bunch underneath?

THE TEST, IN ORDER

  1. Buy a few, not a case. The exact blank, in the exact color you plan to run.
  2. Stitch the actual design. Real size, your normal setup. Not a generic test pattern.
  3. Wash one like the customer will. Home laundry, full cycle.
  4. Read it honestly. Puckering after the wash, uneven shrinkage, color on the thread, edges wandering: any one of them fails the blank.
  5. Keep the passing test. It is your reference when the next batch arrives.

BEFORE IT GOES ON A QUOTE

  • Blank priced at what it costs now, in hand, not from memory.
  • Extra setup this blank needs counted in the job, not absorbed.
  • Customer-supplied blank? Test one of theirs first, and say so in the quote.
  • Cheaper blank requested? Tested, priced, trade-offs stated plainly.
A blank is chosen after the test, never before it.
